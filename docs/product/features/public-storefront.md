# Public storefront

**Status:** live · **Slices:** `src/features/{i18n,properties,enquiries,cinematic-scroll}` · **Routes:** `/[locale]`, `/[locale]/{biens|properties}`, `/[locale]/{biens|properties}/[slug]`, `/[locale]/{a-propos|about}`, `/[locale]/contact`, `/[locale]/legal/[doc]`
**Spec history:** specs/004-belso-public (shipped 2026-08-17)

## What it does (user terms)

A visitor lands on the cinematic scene, describes the property they want in a search field at its centre, and gets a results page with their own words shown back and a count. Each result carries a photograph, its district, its price in the currency it was listed in with an approximate conversion beside it, and enough facts to judge at a glance. They can reorder by price or by how recently a property was listed.

Opening a property gives the photography as a browsable gallery, the full description, key facts and reference, similar properties, and an enquiry form that already knows which property they are looking at. Sending it confirms on screen, naming the property.

The whole site is in French and English, with the language in the address; switching keeps the visitor on the page they were reading and the choice is remembered. Contact and three legal documents are reachable from the footer everywhere.

## How it works

- **Language lives in the URL.** `src/core/i18n.ts` owns the locale list, the default (`fr`), and the translated segment map — `/fr/biens/…` and `/en/properties/…` are the same route. It sits in `core`, not the slice, because `src/proxy.ts` needs it and middleware may not import a feature.
- **`src/proxy.ts`** detects the locale, redirects bare paths, rewrites the translated segment onto the real directory path, and **composes with** `updateSession` rather than replacing it — replacing it signs the visitor out on every translated URL.
- **`features/properties`** — `types.ts` mirrors the future database shape; `repository.ts` is a `server-only` seam over fixtures that Phase 2 swaps for Supabase by replacing one module; `lib.ts` holds the pure logic (translation fallback, the matcher, sort comparators, similarity scoring) so most acceptance criteria are provable without a browser.
- **`features/enquiries`** — a zod schema, a painted-door Server Action that validates like the real thing and persists nothing, and the form. The action returns error _keys_, never sentences, because it cannot know the page's language.
- **`features/i18n`** — `fr.ts` is the source of truth and `en.ts` is typed against it, so adding a French key fails the build until it is translated. A missing string cannot reach a page.
- **`app`** — two root layouts via route groups (`(storefront)`, `(system)`) so `<html lang>` can name the actual language; a nested `(content)` group carries the header and footer for every page except the home scene, which supplies its own.
- **`features/cinematic-scroll`** — two beats, hero then about sheet, over a 2400px runway. Everything the scene says arrives as a `CinematicCopy` prop, because the slice may not reach the i18n slice.

## Decisions & gotchas

- **2026-08-19 — The home page is six sections, and the three new ones write themselves from existing content.** _Le bien du moment_ takes its heading, standfirst, price and figures from one listing — no copy of its own beyond a name and a link label, so nothing there can fall out of date with the catalogue. _Les quartiers_ borrows its lede from the neighbourhood index, and _Vendre_ its statement and steps from the seller’s page, so the promise made on the home page is the one the destination keeps. The page went from roughly 4,200px to 6,279px.
- **2026-08-19 — The featured listing is chosen by rule, not by a flag.** The dearest thing _available_ and for sale: `priceDesc` (which compares converted values — the catalogue mixes dirhams and euros, and the raw number would crown whichever listing was priced in the smaller unit), then the first available sale. The dearest listing overall is an estate under offer, and leading a home page with something a visitor cannot have is a bad first impression. The test states the rule as what it rules out: everything priced above the featured one must be unavailable or a rental.
- **2026-08-19 — Two ink bands now, so the grounds section takes paper.** It was the page’s only dark band when there were three sections; with _Le bien du moment_ and _Vendre_ on ink either side of it, keeping it dark made three of six sections ink and the alternation stopped meaning anything.
- **2026-08-19 — The card has a `quiet` variant, and the home shelf uses it.** `full` is the catalogue card — everything a buyer compares on, reference and publication date included. `quiet` drops that last row for the shelves that are an invitation rather than a search result: the home page and the similar row under a listing. A reference number is what you quote once you are already interested; on a teaser it is administration in a place meant to seduce. Both carry a 16px radius now rather than 8px, which puts the cards in the same family as the hero search and the contact button.
- **2026-08-19 — A label inside a list of links wraps ragged.** The neighbourhood strip set "Les quartiers" as its first `<li>` — one word at a different size, weight and colour in a line of ten place names, and the flex wrap broke around it. It sits above the row now, which is what it always was.
- **2026-08-19 — The footer came down from 465px to 337px, and from 761px to 438px on a phone.** It was a page of its own at the foot of every page — on a 844px screen the old one was most of a screenful. The air came down, the wordmark with it, and the two link columns now sit side by side at every width instead of stacking below `sm`. It also aligns to `--frame-inset` like the header: the two bracket the page, so they cannot disagree about where its edges are — before, the footer sat in a 1280px column while the header spanned the window, and on the full-bleed listings grid the two started 282px apart. Both are asserted in `e2e/home.spec.ts`.
- **2026-08-19 — Resizing the footer re-crops the photograph under it, so its contrast has to be re-swept.** The type is ink on the scene’s sky plate and `object-cover` answers a shorter box with a different slice of it — the same reason the 55% veil was chosen by sweeping viewports rather than measured once. Shrinking it moved every run onto different sky; re-swept at 390/768/1024/1280/1440/1920 it clears everywhere, tightest 5.54:1 at 390px (the veil was originally chosen at 5.05:1). Now a test rather than a one-off check: `e2e/a11y.spec.ts` screenshots the footer with every glyph made transparent and takes the worst pixel under each run.
- **2026-08-19 — The listings grid is the portal layout, in our palette.** Four across, edge to edge, aligned to `--frame-inset` rather than centred in a reading column: a catalogue page answers "how much is there", and a 1280px column on a 1920px screen answers it with three cards and a lot of paper. The card follows the shape every property portal converges on, because it answers the questions in the order a buyer asks them — type and year, price, address, locality, a facts row, then reference and date. Two fields were added to carry it (`builtYear`, `parking`); the facts row is built from what a listing actually has, so land shows its plot rather than four zeros. Kept from ours: the approximate converted price under the asking price (AC-3), and the status badge on the photograph.
- **2026-08-19 — `--frame-margin` / `--frame-pad` / `--frame-inset` live in `globals.css` and nowhere else.** The header and any full-bleed page align to the scene’s frame. They were literals in two files once and drifted, which is how the header came to grow 8px and jump 258px inboard the moment the scene ended.
- **2026-08-19 — A listing date is a calendar day, not an instant.** `new Date("2026-07-28")` parses as midnight **UTC**, so `formatDate` rendered the 27th for every visitor west of Greenwich — a listing dated a day before it was listed, on every card. Pinned to `timeZone: "UTC"`, which prints the day that was written down. Also why the card shows the date rather than "3 days ago": the district pages are statically generated, so a relative label would be baked at build time and drift a day further from the truth every day after.
- **2026-08-19 — The header is one object: same height, same insets, on and off the scene.** Over the film it is inset to the framed world and padded inside that; on an ordinary page it was `mx-auto max-w-7xl px-6` and 8px taller. Leaving the scene therefore moved the wordmark 26px inboard on a 1440px screen and **258px on a 1920px one** — the whole chrome jumped toward the middle and grew at the same moment. Both modes now take their padding from one `--chrome-inset` variable (off the scene it is the frame’s margin _plus_ its padding, since there is no margin to add it to), so they cannot drift apart again. `e2e/home.spec.ts` compares the two rather than pinning numbers: the geometry is free to change, the two modes are not free to disagree about it.
- **2026-08-19 — The language switcher sits beside the wordmark, and that is composition rather than fit.** Next to the contact button it read as part of the call to action and was the thing standing between the button and the right edge; on the left it is a context control beside the identity, and the right side holds exactly one thing, which is the point of putting a button there. The tempting justification — that five locales are planned and would crowd the button — **does not survive measurement**: simulated at five, both placements wrap to three rows on a 390px screen and neither clips (left 120px tall, right 113px). Recorded so nobody moves it back for a reason the numbers do not support, in either direction.
- **2026-08-19 — Select header elements by label, never by position.** The header sweep in `e2e/home.spec.ts` read `navs[0]` as the menu and `navs.at(-1)` as the switcher. Moving the switcher in front of the menu in the DOM swapped both, and the sweep went on passing — measuring the menu’s labels against the switcher’s bounds and calling it fine. A positional selector in a test is a silent assertion about DOM order that nothing else in the repo guarantees.
- **2026-08-19 — Contact is a button at the top right, and it flips rather than fades.** It left the four-link row because a call to action set as the fourth word in a row of four is not one; the footer still lists all four, where it is a directory rather than an ask. The button is the one opaque thing allowed to cross the frame, filled with the scene's own tint so it is cream over the dark sky and ink over the cream sheet. **Blending both ends of a pair against each other is a trap**: the fill travels cream → ink while the label travels ink → cream, so they cross, and at the midpoint they are the same colour. Measured across the tint's full range that was 1.00:1 at t=0.5 and under 4.5:1 for half the transition — a solid pill with no label in it. Stepping the tint at the midpoint locks both ends together at 15.08:1 at every value. A scroll sweep had found only three frames in transition and passed all of them, which is luck rather than evidence: `e2e/a11y.spec.ts` now forces the variable across 0–1 instead.
- **2026-08-19 — Every alt text in the stock pool was written from the file name, and every one of them was wrong.** `card-palm-04` was announced as "the palm grove seen from the terrace" and is a bedroom with an orange bench; `card-sky-11` was "the sky above the Atlas" and is a European apartment block; `grid-pool-dusk` has no pool in it. A sighted visitor sees placeholder photography and reads it as such — a screen-reader user was being read a confident description of a property that does not exist. All thirteen rewritten against the actual frames. What the pool really holds: six bedrooms, two shots of one glazed light well, two of the same 1930s balconies, three European exteriors. Nothing Moroccan.
- **2026-08-19 — The neighbourhood pages carry no photograph, and that is the design.** They were built with one image each, from that same pool. A bedroom captioned "Palmeraie" is not a placeholder a visitor forgives: it is a photograph making a claim about a place, and ten in a grid make ten false claims at once. Set typographically — index number, name in Cormorant, a rule, the lede, the count — they read as an editorial decision rather than a missing asset, which is the honest state of them. `image` goes back on `District` when real photography exists.
- **2026-08-19 — Grouping is on `districtId`, never on the printed district name.** The name is prose that changes language ("Route de l’Ourika" / "Ourika road"), so grouping on it would put one road in two districts depending on who was reading. It also lets the Kasbah listing print its own address while sitting under the medina, which is where it actually is.
- **2026-08-19 — District slugs are not translated, unlike `/biens` vs `/properties`.** They are proper place names — a French buyer and an English one both type "Palmeraie" — so one canonical slug per district means one URL for `hreflang` to point at instead of a per-locale lookup for a word that does not change.
- **2026-08-19 — A full-page screenshot of a page with scroll reveals captures everything below the fold at `opacity: 0`.** The evidence showed a heading over an empty space, and screenshots are how this repo verifies UI — so the primary verification method had been quietly broken since reveals went site-wide. `e2e/utils/shot.ts` now walks the document before a full-page capture and restores the scroll position afterwards. Viewport shots are untouched: on the landing page the scroll position _is_ the scene’s state.
- **2026-08-19 — The header stays at four items, so new pages enter from the footer and in page.** Neighbourhoods and selling are both real destinations, and both lost the argument for header space: five items puts the language switcher off the right edge of a 390px screen. They are reached from the footer, from the listings grid, from the home page, and from every listing’s district line.
- **2026-08-18 — An `hreflang` alternate has to be the other page's canonical, not this page's slug under another prefix.** The detail page built every alternate from the _current_ locale's slug, so the French villa announced its English version as `/en/properties/villa-vue-atlas-palmeraie`. That address resolves — slugs are looked up across locales so a shared French link still opens in English — which is exactly why nothing looked wrong. But the page it reaches names `/en/properties/atlas-view-villa-palmeraie` as its canonical, and an alternate pointing at a page that claims a different canonical is a cluster a crawler discards whole. Found by a test asserting the sitemap and the page agree; `getLocaleSlugs` now answers "this listing's address in every language" once, for both.
- **2026-08-18 — The sitemap is per-locale, and it leaves out anything `noindex`.** It listed one URL for a bilingual site with twelve listings. Every public page is now enumerated in both languages with its translations declared alongside, and listings carry `listedAt` as `lastModified` — the static pages carry none, because build time is not a modification date and would tell a crawler the whole site was rewritten every deploy. The legal documents are absent on purpose: they are `noindex` while the copy is a placeholder, and asking to be indexed in the sitemap while refusing it on the page is a contradiction resolved against the whole site.
- **2026-08-18 — Structured data says what the page can be checked against, and nothing more.** `RealEstateListing` per listing (a plan success condition), with the offer's `businessFunction` separating a sale from a long let and a `referenceQuantity` of one month on the rent, because a rent figure without a period is not a price. `inLanguage` names the language the _text_ is in, not the page's: an untranslated listing shows French prose under an English URL (AC-9), and claiming `en-GB` there is a lie nothing on the page contradicts. Geo coordinates are omitted until the map is built — inventing a point for a private residence is not a rounding error.
- **2026-08-18 — The header wraps to two rows below 640px.** Four links, a wordmark and the language switcher need about 420px of the 336px a 390px phone offers. In one row "À propos" broke onto two lines and the switcher was pushed to x=447 in a 390px viewport — clipped rather than scrollable, so on any phone at or under 414px the language could not be changed at all. Shrinking the type instead recovers roughly 56px of the 84px needed and cheapens the chrome to do it. `order-last` moves the nav to the second line without moving it in the DOM, so reading and tab order are unchanged. Swept at five widths on both header modes in `e2e/home.spec.ts`.
- **2026-08-18 — Anything that starts hidden needs the `<noscript>` guard.** Scroll reveals and the content-page transition both render their hidden start state on the server, so without the `[data-reveal]{opacity:1 !important}` rule in the root shell the site is blank to anyone whose JavaScript did not run — a browser setting, but also a failed chunk or a blocked CDN. It shipped broken once: the reveals carried `data-reveal`, the transition wrapper added afterwards did not, and every content page rendered empty. `e2e/a11y.spec.ts` now loads the site with JS disabled and asserts `opacity: 1`, because `toBeVisible()` passes on a fully transparent element.
- **2026-08-18 — A route with `loading.tsx` renders only its skeleton without JavaScript.** Next swaps a streamed Suspense fallback for the real content using an inline script, so `/[locale]/properties` shows its skeleton and stops. Crawlers execute JS and are unaffected; a no-JS visitor is not. It is a property of streaming rather than of any one page, and it is the second thing `loading.tsx` costs here — see the soft-404 note below.
- **2026-08-18 — The hero search is a labelled block on its own paper, and its placeholder is load-bearing.** That example sentence is the only thing telling a visitor the field reads a description rather than keywords, and it shipped clipped at "Villa moderne à Marraké" — 191px of field for 395px of sentence. The label was `sr-only`, so the one line explaining the field was hidden from everyone who could see. Both are fixed, and `e2e/home.spec.ts` now measures the placeholder against the field at four widths, because the field is sized by a chain of percentages and it re-clipped twice while being fixed.
- **2026-08-18 — `.heroBlock` needs an explicit `width: 100%`.** Both its ancestors are `align-items: flex-start`, so it was shrink-to-fit and every percentage width inside it resolved against its own content: a field declared `min(100%, 470px)` rendered at 375px.
- **2026-08-18 — Small type cannot sit bare on the scene.** Measured over the hero's lower-left corner, the search label came out at 1.46:1 and its hint at 1.02:1. No colour fixes it — that corner runs from a dark building to a bright sky inside one frame, so cream dies on the sky and ink dies on the building. The big lede survives only because it is 53px and needs 3:1. Anything smaller needs its own surface.
- **2026-08-17 — The footer is the scene's sky plate under a 55% paper veil, with ink type.** The page opens on that sky and ends on it. A deep-brown scrim came first, so cream type could sit on it — it held contrast but hid the thing it was carrying. Ink on the plate is the same move the hero makes with its lede.
- **2026-08-17 — Contrast over a photograph must be swept across viewports, not measured once.** `object-cover` answers a wide short box and a tall narrow one with completely different crops: the unveiled plate measured 4.6:1 at 1440px and 1.12:1 at 390px, where the whole picture is in frame and the copyright line lands on the road at its foot. The veil was chosen by sweeping 390→1920 (45% → four failures, 50% → one, 55% → clear, tightest 5.05:1). Two traps in the measuring itself: reading `getComputedStyle(el).backgroundColor` is meaningless once the ground is an image — the check screenshots the footer with every glyph made transparent, decodes it on a canvas in-page, and takes the worst pixel in each run's box; and computed colours resolve to `oklab(...)` here, so a naive numeric parse reads `oklab(0.184 0.013 55.5)` as RGB and reports every line at 1.01:1. Element `opacity` is invisible to a check that reads `color`, so quiet type fades the colour, never the element.
- **2026-08-17 — The scene ends on the about sheet; what follows is an ordinary page.** It used to run six beats over a 6600px runway — split frames, a residences bridge, an amenities panel, a sliding card deck over a photo collage. Each spoke a different motion language and together they held the scroll for four thousand pixels to say what three static sections say better. The runway is now 2400px, the about sheet has no exit (the page carries it away when the sticky stage releases), and ~630 lines of CSS, the pointer-parallax loop and the whole slider went with them.
- **2026-08-17 — Navigation is routes, never scroll positions.** `#about`, `#residences` and `#amenities` were markers placed down the scene's runway: not shareable, not indexable, meaningless from any other page, and broken outright from all of them. `/[locale]/{a-propos|about}` is now a real page and the header is the same four links everywhere.
- **2026-08-17 — The header leaves scene mode on the scene's box, not a scroll number.** `scrollY < MOTION.runway` reads as equivalent and is wrong by a full viewport — the sticky stage is still pinned at the end of the runway, so the scene occupies `100vh + runway`. The header spent that last screenful transparent over ordinary page content, printing its legibility halo as grey smudges around the wordmark on cream. It now measures `#scene`'s bottom against the shared `CHROME_BAND`.
- **2026-08-17 — A `loading.tsx` anywhere above a route turns its 404 into a soft 200.** The shell streams before `notFound()` throws, so the status is already sent; the page renders correctly and returns 200 to every crawler. The listings skeleton therefore lives in `properties/(index)/`, which does not wrap `[slug]`, and the detail, contact and legal routes ship no `loading.tsx`. If one of them ever needs streaming, put `<Suspense>` _inside_ the page below the `notFound()` decision — never above it. **This contradicts `.claude/rules/app-router.md`**, which mandates `loading.tsx` on every feature route.
- **2026-08-17 — Next strips its own router headers before `proxy` runs.** `rsc`, `next-router-prefetch` and `next-router-segment-prefetch` are absent there, so a prefetch cannot be detected that way. This mattered because the locale switcher sits in every header, so Next prefetches the _other_ language's URL and the proxy counted it as a choice — silently overwriting the visitor's language. The proxy now records a locale only on `sec-fetch-dest: document`, and the switcher writes the cookie itself on click.
- **2026-08-17 — Prices sort on a converted value, not the raw number.** The fixtures mix MAD and EUR; comparing digits ranked a 12.8M MAD villa above a €3.9M estate. `similarityScore` deliberately does _not_ convert — there a stale rate shifts weighting invisibly, while here not converting produces a visibly false order.
- **2026-08-17 — `localizeProperty` lists every field instead of spreading.** A spread carries `translations` onto the object handed to components, and any component holding that map is one `.en` away from bypassing the fallback rule and rendering a blank description.
- **2026-08-17 — `repository.ts` is exported from the slice barrel** despite being `server-only`, because the boundary rule admits one entry point and changing it needs an ADR. Consequence: importing anything from `@/features/properties` into a client component fails the build naming `server-only`, not the barrel.
- **2026-08-17 — Card focus rings live on the card, not the link.** The stretched title link suppresses its own outline (it would draw a box around the title mid-card); the `<article>` rings itself via `has-[a:focus-visible]`. Suppressing without replacing left the entire grid invisible to keyboard focus.
- **2026-08-17 — `fr-MA` groups thousands with `.`**, so prices render `12.000.000 MAD`. A French-from-France buyer may read that as wrong. Pinned by a test; switching to `fr-FR` is a one-line change to `localeTag`.

## Known gaps

- **Legal copy is placeholder.** The GDPR-expected sections exist and each is marked as unwritten; pages are `noindex` until real text arrives. No owner has been named.
- **Enquiries go nowhere.** Painted door by design (`docs/process/painted-door.md`); the back-office inbox is spec 003. The action is unthrottled, which is harmless while it persists nothing and must be rate-limited before it does not.
- **The language switcher sends a listing to a non-canonical address.** `switchLocalePath` keeps the trailing slug, so switching to English on a French listing lands on `/en/properties/<french-slug>` — which renders correctly, and is what AC-1 asks for, but is not that page's canonical. `LocaleSwitcher` accepts `slugOverrides` for exactly this; the switcher is rendered by the layout's header, so a per-page override needs plumbing that the header does not have yet. The `hreflang` set and the sitemap are correct regardless.
- **Content and photography are fixtures.** Twenty Marrakech listings across ten districts, two per district. Thirteen stock frames stand in for real photography, and they are the binding constraint on the whole storefront — six of the thirteen are bedrooms, none is Moroccan, and no page can show a pool, a courtyard, an olive tree or the Atlas because no such frame exists. Real photography unblocks the neighbourhood pages, the gallery repetition and the alt text at once.
